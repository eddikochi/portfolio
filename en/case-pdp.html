Data-driven Product Page Redesign

Behavioral diagnosis via Microsoft Clarity and restructuring of the product page (PDP) for IBCMED (Inspirali Pós), from discovery to handoff with the product and technology teams. The work was to understand real behavior before touching the layout — and discover that the bottleneck wasn't the CTA, but the transition between validation and action.

02 — Context

The decision page of the journey

The product page (PDP) for IBCMED (Inspirali Pós) postgraduate courses is the decision page of the journey: the user has moved past generic curiosity and is evaluating value, reducing risk and seeking clarity before acting. Its goal is to generate qualified leads — the primary conversion is form submission.

The existing PDP was generic and identical for all courses. Two structural problems limited it: each modality of the same specialization (hybrid, in-person, online) generated a separate page, multiplying the number of PDPs on the site and cluttering the catalog itself; and the content was presented in accordion format, forcing users through a sequence of clicks to access information essential to their decision. Performance readings, even so, couldn't explain why conversion didn't match traffic.

03 — Problem

Relevant traffic, conversion that doesn't correspond

The page received relevant traffic, but conversion didn't correspond. Understanding real behavior before touching the layout was missing — effort was being invested without measurable impact, with hypotheses based on assumption, not evidence.

Central insight

The bottleneck wasn't the CTA, but the transition between validation and action.

04 — My role

From behavioral diagnosis to handoff with tech

I led the behavioral diagnosis and discovery of the new PDP, and acted as the bridge with the product and technology teams in the handoff and component optimization:

  • Behavioral analysis via Microsoft Clarity (90 days, mobile and desktop).
  • Diagnosis of navigation patterns, scroll, clicks and friction (rage and dead clicks).
  • Discovery and structuring of the new PDP, from the objection map to the user flow.
  • Handoff and execution follow-through with product and technology, optimizing components.
  • Direct implementation of part of the changes in AEM.
05 — Process

Separating validation from action to find the real bottleneck

  1. DiagnosisBehavioral analysis on Clarity
  2. RepositioningThe right problem: validation → action
  3. RedesignDiscovery and governance map
  4. ProductionImplementation in AEM

Diagnosis with criteria. The Clarity analysis followed criteria to avoid conclusions from isolated cases: analyze multiple sessions, diagnose patterns not exceptions, separate mobile from desktop and isolate the specific page. The sample focused on the highest-traffic course, across short, medium and long sessions, with and without clicks.

Separating validation from action. The key point of the diagnosis was treating scroll and reading as real micro-conversions — validation signals — and separating them from the final action (form submission). This revealed two recurring behavior profiles:

Already decided

  • Goes straight to the form, little scroll, early conversion.
  • The site merely executes a decision made outside it.

In validation

  • Reads a lot, consistent scroll, long pauses on "who the course is for", "practical classes" and "faculty".
  • Uses the site to decide if the course is right for them.

The central insight. The CTA wasn't the decision trigger — it functioned as the final execution mechanism for those already decided. Conversion was the result of confidence built throughout navigation, not an isolated stimulus. The form wasn't the problem and the CTA wasn't confusing: after consuming the validation content, the user received no explicit guidance on the next step — and it was in that transition, not the button, that conversion was lost.

From diagnosis to redesign. The discovery of the new PDP started from this diagnosis — objection map, user flow and final structure — and resulted in a component governance map that defines each page fold (function, authoring, fixed structure and reuse across PDPs). The new structure resolved both original bottlenecks: consolidated modalities of a specialization into a single page, eliminating PDP multiplication, and abandoned the accordion in favor of more direct and scannable content, aligned with technical SEO and the existing design system structure.

06 — Key decisions

The choices that guided the redesign

Diagnose before redesigning

Behavioral analysis (Clarity) before any layout change — evidence-based decision, not assumption.

Consolidate modalities into a single PDP

One page per specialization, with variations (hybrid, in-person, online) within it — fewer PDPs, clean catalog, simpler governance and maintenance.

Reduce accordion friction

Deliver validation content in a direct and scannable format, without the click sequence the accordion required.

Scroll and reading as micro-conversions

Measure validation, not just the final conversion, to understand where the journey really broke.

Context to the CTA after validation

Reduce hesitation by guiding the user from "I get it" to "I want it".

PDP as a component system

Move from a generic page to a governance map that defines each fold, reusable across courses.

09 — Impact

From "the CTA doesn't convert" to the right problem

The impact was qualitative and process-driven:

  • The redesign decision became guided by behavioral evidence, not assumption.
  • The diagnosis repositioned the problem: it stopped being "the CTA doesn't convert" and became "there's no transition between validation and action" — a different problem that changes what you build.
  • The PDP moved from a generic page to a governed and reusable component structure across courses, with modalities consolidated into a single page.
  • The redesign proposal was approved on first presentation to the board.
  • The redesign was implemented in production, with part of the implementations done directly by me in AEM.

The redesign was implemented in production; conversion measurement post-implementation continued with the team.

10 — Learnings

What stayed

  • Understanding behavior before touching the layout avoids optimizing the wrong element: the form and the CTA weren't the problem.
  • Micro-conversions (scroll, reading) tell the story that the final conversion rate hides.
  • The most valuable role wasn't designing the screen, but translating behavioral data into product decisions and carrying them through to tech execution.
11 — Next steps

Possible evolution

Projected continuity.

  • Measure conversion on the new PDP in production, validating the hypothesis that a longer but more qualified flow improves lead quality.
  • Refine desktop breakpoints, identified as the main remaining technical risk.
