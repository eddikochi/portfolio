02 — Context

The decision page of the journey

The product page for the IBCMED (Inspirali Pós) graduate courses is the decision page of the journey: the user is past generic curiosity and is now weighing value, reducing risk and looking for clarity before acting. The goal is to generate qualified leads — the main conversion is submitting the interest form.

The existing PDP was generic and identical for every course. Two structural problems limited it: each format of the same specialization (hybrid, in-person, online) generated a separate page, multiplying the number of PDPs and cluttering the catalog; and the content came in an accordion format, requiring a sequence of clicks to reach information essential to the decision.

03 — Problem

Relevant traffic, conversion that doesn’t match

The page received relevant traffic, but conversion didn’t keep up. What was missing was understanding real behavior before touching the layout — effort was being spent with no measurable impact, on hypotheses based on assumption, not evidence.

Core insight

The bottleneck wasn’t the CTA, but the transition between validation and action.

04 — My role

From behavioral diagnosis to handoff with tech

I ran the behavioral diagnosis and the discovery of the new PDP, and acted as the bridge with product and technology in the handoff and component optimization:

  • Behavioral analysis via Microsoft Clarity (90 days, mobile and desktop).
  • Diagnosis of navigation, scroll and click patterns and frictions (rage and dead clicks).
  • Discovery and structuring of the new PDP, from the objection map to the user flow.
  • Handoff and follow-up of execution with product and technology.
  • Implementation of part of the changes directly in AEM.
05 — Process

Separate validation from action to find the real bottleneck

  1. DiagnosisBehavioral analysis in Clarity
  2. ReframingThe right problem: validation → action
  3. RedesignDiscovery and governance map
  4. ProductionImplementation in AEM

Diagnosis with criteria. The Clarity analysis followed criteria to avoid concluding from an isolated case: multiple sessions, patterns rather than exceptions, mobile separate from desktop and one page in isolation. The cut was made on the highest-traffic course, across short, medium and long sessions, with and without a click.

Separating validation from action. The key move was treating scroll and reading as microconversions — validation signals — and separating them from the final action (submitting the form). That revealed two recurring profiles:

Already decided

  • Goes straight to the form, little scroll, early conversion.
  • The site merely executes a decision made outside it.

In validation

  • Reads a lot, consistent scroll, pauses on “who the course is for”, “hands-on classes” and “faculty”.
  • Uses the site to decide whether the course is for them.

The core insight. The CTA wasn’t the decision trigger — it worked as a final execution mechanism for those already decided. Conversion came from the trust built throughout the navigation. After consuming the validation content, the user got no explicit guidance on the next step — and it was in that transition, not in the button, that conversion was lost.

From diagnosis to redesign. The new PDP discovery started from that diagnosis — objection map, user flow and final structure — and resulted in a component governance map that defines each section of the page. The new structure solved the two root bottlenecks: it consolidated a specialization’s formats into a single page and dropped the accordion in favor of direct, scannable content, aligned with technical SEO and the existing design system.

06 — Key decisions

The choices that guided the redesign

Diagnose before redesigning

Behavioral analysis (Clarity) before any layout change — decision by evidence.

Consolidate formats into one PDP

One page per specialization, with the variations inside it — fewer PDPs, a clean catalog, simpler maintenance.

Reduce the accordion friction

Direct, scannable validation content, without the sequence of clicks.

Scroll and reading as microconversions

Measure validation, not only the final conversion, to find where the journey broke.

Context for the CTA after validation

Lead the user from “I get it” to “I want to do it”.

PDP as a component system

A governance map that defines each section, reusable across courses.

09 — Impact

From “the CTA doesn’t convert” to the right problem

The impact was qualitative and about process:

  • The redesign decision became driven by behavioral evidence, not assumption.
  • The diagnosis reframed the problem: from “the CTA doesn’t convert” to “there’s no transition between validation and action” — a different problem, which changes what you build.
  • The PDP went from a generic page to a governed, reusable component structure, with the formats consolidated into a single page.
  • The proposal was approved on the first pass by leadership.
  • The redesign was implemented in production, with part of the work done by me in AEM.

Post-implementation conversion measurement continued with the team after I left — I didn’t follow the final numbers. What I stand behind here is the diagnosis, the approved redesign and the delivery to production.

10 — Learnings

What stayed

  • Understanding behavior before touching the layout avoids optimizing the wrong element: the form and the CTA weren’t the problem.
  • Microconversions (scroll, reading) tell the story the final conversion rate hides.
  • The most valuable role wasn’t designing the screen, but translating behavioral data into a product decision and taking it to execution with tech.
