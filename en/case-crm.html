CRM · email journeys · lead nurturing

CRM as an engine for lead relationship and conversion

Structuring and operating email and multichannel CRM journeys for lead capture and nurturing at a medical postgraduate institution. The work was to turn ad-hoc blasts into a communication flow that reacts to lead behavior — from first contact to enrollment — and make the operation measurable.

02 — Context

Capture across many channels, relationship without a structure

The institution captured postgraduate and continuing education leads through multiple channels, but communication with those bases had no structured flow. The relationship depended on ad-hoc blasts, without a journey that nurtured the lead from first contact to enrollment — and without continuity afterwards.

03 — Problem

Ad-hoc blasts, no nurturing and no measurement

The CRM operation had weaknesses that limited conversion:

  • Low open rates and no structured lead nurturing.
  • Results read only through spreadsheets, with no consolidated measurement.
  • Email pieces (EMM) with poor performance.
  • Lack of alignment between CRM and Sales teams.
  • No post-enrollment nurturing — the relationship stopped at conversion.
Central insight

A flow that reacts to behavior converts more than blast volume.

04 — My role

From piece to performance: operating CRM end-to-end

I worked in CRM operations, from building pieces to reading performance:

  • Building email pieces in modular HTML.
  • Assembling and dispatching journeys in Salesforce.
  • Structuring the communication flows together with the team.
  • Reading performance and presenting results via dashboard.
  • Bridge between CRM and Sales teams.
05 — Constraints

What limited the work

  • Tool limitations available.
  • Dependency on other areas for content release and definitions.
  • Short execution timelines.
06 — Process

From isolated blasts to a flow that reacts to behavior

The core of the work was structuring a communication flow that guided the lead through stages, instead of isolated blasts.

  1. WelcomeInitial flow with name personalization
  2. Branch by open"Did they open the email?" defines the next step
  3. NurturingSequence that progressively warms up the lead
  4. ConversionForm submission and enrollment

The initial welcome flow served all leads who hadn't yet indicated specific interest, with name personalization. From there, the journey branched by behavior: the trigger "did they open the email?" defined the next step — those who opened continued in the nurturing sequence; those who didn't received a reinforcement SMS and re-invitation.

The email sequence (EMM) was designed to progressively warm up the lead — welcome, institution highlights, course modalities and finally structure and testimonials —, with content division by area from the third send. CRM and Sales activation happened in a coordinated way, resolving the previous misalignment.

Performance reading followed a metric hierarchy: open rate → read rate → click. A/B tests were applied first on email subject lines and then on CTAs, always with defined hypothesis, variation and result analysis.

Beyond the nurturing flow, the operation included email and WhatsApp blasts for event alerts and promotions.

07 — Key decisions

The choices that structured the operation

Behavior-branched flow

Replace ad-hoc blasts with a journey that reacts to the lead's open — continues in nurturing or triggers SMS reinforcement.

"Ideal flow" design

Target journey project, structured with the team, as the operational evolution reference.

Newsletter

Recurring relationship channel with the base.

Checkout and abandoned cart

Purchase flow with cart recovery, connecting CRM and direct conversion.

Dashboard-based reading

From spreadsheet analysis to dashboard (Power BI), with clear metric hierarchy.

Systematic A/B tests

Testing subject lines and CTAs as an ongoing practice, not a one-off.

10 — Impact

From ad-hoc blasts to a measurable operation

The impact was qualitative and operational — no fabricated metrics, but changes the operation felt day to day:

Before

  • Ad-hoc blasts to the entire base.
  • No structured lead nurturing.
  • Results read via manual spreadsheets.
  • CRM and Sales misaligned.

After

  • Structured and measurable flow.
  • Behavior branching — engaged and non-engaged leads treated differently.
  • Continuous performance dashboard (open, read, click).
  • CRM and Sales coordinated by the journey itself.
11 — Learnings

What stayed

  • A flow that reacts to behavior converts better than blast volume.
  • Consolidated measurement (dashboard) changes the conversation with other areas: decisions stop being opinions and become data readings.
  • Most of the gain came from structuring what already existed in dispersed form, not from adding complexity.
12 — Next steps

Possible evolution

Projected vision, not implemented — I left the sector before this stage.

  • Post-enrollment nurturing: the clearest gap in the operation. The flow ended at conversion; the natural next step would be structuring the relationship with the enrolled student — onboarding, recurrence and repurchase of new courses.
