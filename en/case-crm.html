CRM · email journeys · lead nurturing

CRM as an engine for relationship and lead conversion

Structuring and running multichannel email and CRM journeys for lead capture and nurturing at a medical graduate-studies institution. The work was turning one-off blasts into a flow that reacts to lead behavior — from first contact to enrollment — and making the operation measurable.

02 — Context

Capture across many channels, relationship without a flow

The institution captured graduate-studies and continuing-education leads through multiple channels, but communication with those bases had no structured flow. The relationship relied on one-off blasts, with no journey to nurture the lead from first contact to enrollment — and no continuity after it.

03 — Problem

One-off blasts, no nurturing or measurement

The CRM operation had weaknesses that limited conversion:

  • Low open rates and no structured nurturing of leads.
  • Results read only through spreadsheets, with no consolidated measurement.
  • Email pieces (EMM) with low performance.
  • Lack of alignment between CRM and Sales.
  • No post-enrollment nurturing — the relationship stopped at conversion.
Core insight

A flow that reacts to behavior converts more than blast volume.

04 — My role

From the piece to performance: running CRM end to end

I ran the CRM operation, from building the pieces to reading performance:

  • Building the email pieces in modular HTML.
  • Assembling and sending the journeys in Salesforce.
  • Structuring the communication flows together with the team.
  • Reading performance and presenting results via a dashboard.
  • Bridge between CRM and Sales.
05 — Constraints

What limited the work

  • Limitations of the available tools.
  • Dependency on other areas to release content and definitions.
  • Short execution deadlines.
06 — Process

From isolated blasts to a flow that reacts to behavior

  1. WelcomeInitial flow with name personalization
  2. Branching“Opened the email?” defines the next step
  3. NurturingSequence that warms up the lead
  4. ConversionForm submission and enrollment

The welcome flow served all leads with no specific interest indicated yet, with name personalization. From there, the journey branched by behavior: the “opened the email?” trigger defined the next step — those who opened continued in nurturing; those who didn’t received a reminder SMS and re-invite.

The email sequence was designed to warm the lead up progressively — welcome, differentiators, formats and, finally, structure and testimonials — split by area from the third send on. Performance reading followed a hierarchy: open → read → click. A/B tests were applied first to subject lines and then to CTAs. Beyond nurturing, the operation included email and WhatsApp blasts for events and promotions.

07 — Key decisions

The choices that structured the operation

Flow with behavior branching

Replace one-off blasts with a journey that reacts to the open — continues in nurturing or triggers SMS.

Designing the “ideal flow”

A target-journey design, structured with the team, as a reference for evolution.

Newsletter

A recurring relationship channel with the base.

Checkout and abandoned cart

A purchase flow with recovery, connecting CRM and direct conversion.

Dashboard reading

From spreadsheet to dashboard (Power BI), with a clear metric hierarchy.

Systematic A/B tests

Testing subject and CTA as a continuous, not one-off, practice.

09 — Impact

From one-off blast to measurable operation

The impact was qualitative and operational — no fabricated metrics, but changes the operation felt day to day:

Before

  • One-off blasts to the whole base.
  • No structured lead nurturing.
  • Results read through manual spreadsheets.
  • CRM and Sales misaligned.

After

  • A structured, measurable flow.
  • Behavior branching — engaged and unengaged handled differently.
  • A continuous performance dashboard.
  • CRM and Sales coordinated by the journey.
10 — Learnings

What stayed

  • A flow that reacts to behavior converts better than blast volume.
  • Consolidated measurement changes the conversation with other areas: decisions stop being opinion and become data reading.
  • Most of the gain came from structuring what already existed in a scattered way, not from adding complexity.

I left the department before structuring post-enrollment nurturing — the clearest gap in the operation and the natural next step.
